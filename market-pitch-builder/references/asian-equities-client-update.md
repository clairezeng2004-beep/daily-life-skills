# User-approved example: Asian equities client update

Use this example for tone, causal signposting, detail level and structure when preparing a short multi-news institutional-client update. The user supplied this version on 21 October 2026. Only minor grammar was corrected: “The valuation” to “the valuation” after a semicolon, and “MSCI’s decision to Indonesia” to “MSCI’s decision on Indonesia”.

The numbers and events are historical example content, not current facts or standing trade recommendations. Verify dates, market moves, causal claims and source access before reusing them. ## Example

Asian equities：市场更新

韩国：融资成本与竞争加剧 → 投资者担心 AI 利润持续性。
中国：出口增长强劲 → AI 电子产品与芯片涨价贡献明显。
印尼：指数改革 → 市场期待避免 MSCI 剔除，但结果未定。
配置：选择有技术优势的芯片股；偏好能保住利润的出口商；印尼等待确认。

What happened and why:
First, Korea’s Kospi fell 3.7% on Tuesday, and Elice dropped 32% on its debut. That’s because investors worried that higher borrowing costs and Chinese competition could hurt future AI profits.

Second, China’s September exports rose 36.3%. That’s partly because AI electronics shipments grew and chip prices rose.

Third, Indonesia rebounded about 5%. That’s because investors hoped new index rules would help avoid MSCI exclusion. MSCI has not yet confirmed that the changes are enough.

Positioning:
For AI, I’d favour established chipmakers, where advanced manufacturing gives the business a competitive advantage. But strong earnings alone are not enough; the valuation still needs to make sense.

In China, I prefer exporters that can protect margins despite tariffs and costs. Higher export values are encouraging, but I want evidence that profits also improve. I will wait for stronger household spending before adding consumer stocks in China.

For Indonesia, I think the rebound makes sense, but I will wait for MSCI’s response. A recovery in share prices does not remove the risk that index funds may have to sell.

Watch next:
My priorities are chip margins and customer orders, Chinese exporters’ profits and retail sales, and MSCI’s decision on Indonesia.
