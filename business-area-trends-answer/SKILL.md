# Business Area Trends Answer

Use this skill when the user asks application or interview questions such as "How is this business area changing?", "Why are these changes happening?", "Where are the opportunities and challenges?", or asks for industry dynamics for a specific bank programme. It is designed for finance roles such as Markets/Sales & Trading, Capital Markets, Private Bank/Wealth, Asset Management, Banking, Securities Services and related graduate or summer programmes.

For final English prose, follow the language style of `application-answer-polisher`: polished but human, specific, non-AI-sounding, and faithful to the user's story. This skill owns the trend thesis and evidence logic; `application-answer-polisher` owns the default prose feel. Do not require the user to provide both skill links.

## Core Standard

The answer should have one clear, defensible thesis about the business area. Do not produce a broad survey of every trend in finance. A strong answer explains:

1. what is changing in the actual business area;
2. why that change is happening;
3. what opportunity it creates for clients, the bank or junior talent;
4. what challenge or trade-off comes with it.

If a draft could be reused for several business areas by swapping the title, it is too generic.

## Define The Business Area First

Start from the JD, not from the user's shorthand.

- "Wealth - Private Bank" means the business area is Private Bank/Wealth; Hong Kong is location context.
- "Banking - Capital Markets" means Capital Markets; do not drift into general Investment Banking or Sales & Trading unless the JD says the role sits across them.
- "Markets" or "Sales & Trading" should focus on client risk, liquidity, execution, market structure or product context, not generic comments about fast-paced markets.

If the prompt says "this business area" and the JD is available, extract the business area and state it before drafting.

## Research Rules

Use current, credible sources because market structure, regulation and programme context change. Prefer:

- the supplied JD and official programme page;
- official bank business pages, annual reports and investor-day materials;
- regulator, exchange or central bank releases;
- reputable market-structure or industry reports for external context.

Keep evidence honest:

- Label what the source directly says as fact.
- Label the answer's interpretation as inference.
- Do not turn a source fact into a stronger claim. For example, a bank's global presence does not prove a desk has an exclusive advantage.
- Do not use a precise number unless it changes the argument.
- Avoid comparative claims such as "only", "unique", "largest" or "leading" unless the source directly supports the comparison.

When useful, maintain an internal evidence matrix:

| Claim | Source fact | Date/location | Why it matters | Fact or inference |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Find A Narrow Thesis

Avoid defaulting to "AI, digitalisation, regulation and volatility are changing the industry." These are often true, but they read like a generic AI answer unless tied to a specific business mechanism.

Useful thesis patterns:

- **Capital Markets:** The business is becoming more sensitive to market timing. Clients still need capital, but rate expectations, investor appetite and issuance windows can change quickly.
- **Private Bank / Wealth:** The business is shifting from managing wealth for one individual to advising globally mobile families across generations.
- **Sales & Trading / Markets:** The role is moving away from execution alone toward helping clients interpret liquidity, risk and market structure in a specific product or local market.
- **Asset Management:** The business is shifting toward clearer outcome design for clients, where performance, risk, liquidity and transparency must be explained in a way that fits the client's mandate.

These are starting points, not templates. Narrow them using the target bank, office, client segment and current market evidence.

## Opportunities And Challenges

Do not list opportunities and challenges as separate buzzwords. Pair each opportunity with the friction that makes it meaningful.

Examples:

- A stronger IPO market creates opportunity, but timing and investor sentiment still matter.
- More electronic execution improves efficiency, but stressed markets still require judgement.
- More globally mobile wealth creates demand for advice, but trust and privacy become harder to maintain.
- More data can sharpen decisions, but it can also encourage overconfidence if market context is ignored.

Prefer "opportunity plus trade-off" over "opportunities include A, B and C."

## Citi-Specific Lessons From Prior Use

When the target is Citi, do not rely on "Citi is global" as the main point. That is usually true of peer banks too. Use Citi facts only when they alter the business mechanism.

- For Capital Markets, Citi's cross-border institutional focus is relevant only if tied to how a client chooses financing route, funding currency, investor base or market window.
- For Private Bank, the global angle should be about internationally mobile families, cross-border wealth and next-generation decisions, not a generic global network.
- For Markets/Sales & Trading, connect Citi's local market or product context to client risk management; do not write a broad answer about technology and volatility.

If the example would work equally well for another bank or another region, narrow it further or drop it.

## Writing Style

Write like a strong candidate with a real view, not a market report.

- Apply the `application-answer-polisher` style target when drafting the final answer.
- Use one thesis sentence early.
- Keep paragraphs short and purposeful.
- Prefer one concrete example over three broad nouns.
- Avoid template contrasts: "not only..., but also...", "not limited to...", "rather than..., it is...", "more than just..." and repeated "both...and..." structures.
- Avoid list-like rhythm. If a sentence reads like "A, B and C" or "to X, Y and Z", keep the most relevant detail or rewrite around a situation, trade-off or judgement.
- Use cautious language for inference: "This suggests", "I see", "from my understanding", "one implication is".
- Keep citations or source notes outside the submitted answer unless the user asks to include them.

## Deliverable Shape

Unless the user asks for only the final draft, provide:

1. **Recommended thesis** in one sentence.
2. **Evidence basis** with source links and a clear distinction between facts and inferences.
3. **Final answer** within the word limit, written as submission-ready prose.
4. **Accuracy notes** if any claim has been narrowed to avoid overclaiming.

Final quality gate:

- [ ] The business area is correctly defined from the JD.
- [ ] The answer has one specific thesis, not a trend list.
- [ ] Every material claim is supported or clearly framed as inference.
- [ ] Opportunities and challenges are connected to the same business mechanism.
- [ ] The wording would not work unchanged for three peer banks or three different business areas.
- [ ] The prose avoids heavy parallel phrasing and AI-style contrasts.
