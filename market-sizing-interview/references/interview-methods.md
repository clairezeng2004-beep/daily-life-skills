# Interview method sources

Public pages reviewed on 21 October 2026. These are interview-preparation providers' own materials, not endorsements or guarantees of interview outcomes. The summaries below are original paraphrases. The skill's output format and first-person English rules come from the user's preferences, not these providers.

## MyConsultingCoach: market sizing, estimates and segmentation

https://www.myconsultingcoach.com/case-interview-how-to-segment

Supports: define the target, choose demand or supply, segment into non-overlapping groups that cover the scope, and sense-check the estimate. The skill adapts this into short formulas and explicit units rather than prescribing one diagram. Do not copy the article's demographic shortcuts into every case.

## Crafting Cases: Master Market Sizing, public course overview

https://students.craftingcases.com/courses/master-market-sizing

Supports: explain why an approach fits, justify assumptions, select useful segmentation, and reality-check the result. Only the public overview was reviewed; no claim is made to reproduce paid lessons or a proprietary framework. Apply these principles by replacing opaque ratios with explainable customer behaviour or operating assumptions.

## CaseCoach: Case Interview Preparation Guide

https://casecoach.com/b/case-interview-preparation/

Supports: clarify the problem, use a structure tailored to the question, communicate calculations clearly, and synthesise the answer naturally. This is broader case-interview guidance, not a source for coffee purchase rates or airport statistics.

## How to use the sources

The workflow is a synthesis, not a named provider's official method. Use the sources to improve reasoning, not to claim an estimate is factually verified. Framework-only practice omits arithmetic results by user preference; a full numerical interview answer still needs calculation and a conclusion when requested. Refresh links if asked for current resources. Actual population, airport operations, store counts, vehicle penetration, and illustrative purchase rates require separate evidence if used as facts.
